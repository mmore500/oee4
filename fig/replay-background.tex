\caption{%
\textbf{Self context selects for genetic complexity maintenance in case study strain.}
\footnotesize
To identify the role of biotic context on maintenance of evolved complexity in the case study strain, we performed short one-stint replay trials on strain populations from stints 33-100 ($n=67$).
Replays were conducted in either ecological context (comprising contemporary population from the co-evolved ``background'' strain) or self context (comprising the case study strain's own contemporary population), with endpoint genetic complexity measured via single-site knockout fitness assays (panel \ref{fig:replay-background:design}).
To enhance standing variation and weaken inherent self-context effects, seeded foreground genomes were subjected to moderate mutagenization, dosed to maintain a minority of genomes functionally intact.
At this dose level, replays in self-context maintained significantly higher genetic complexity than ecological context (Mann-Whitney $U$ test, panel \ref{fig:replay-background:moderatedose}).
As a control, this effect did not manifest under stronger mutagenization expected to substantially degrade all seeded genomes (Mann-Whitney $U$ test, panel \ref{fig:replay-background:highdose}).
\unskip\protect\footnotemark
In earlier timepoint replays, self-context exerted no effect on genetic complexity at stints 1-24, and only a marginal effect at stints 25-34 (Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:replay-background-early}).
