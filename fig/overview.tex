\caption{
\textbf{Overview of digital multicell model.}
\footnotesize
Population of digital organisms inhabit cells on toroidal grid, competing for available space.
Digital organisms may form multicell groups --- shown as color-coded patches ($a$).
An occupied cell replicates by replacing a chosen neighbor with its own daughter.
Parent cells may choose to grow their existing multicell group ($c$) --- or found a new group ($d$).
Cells execute heritable genetic programs analogous to biological genomes ($e$).
Program instructions may sense local simulation state, dispatch cell-cell messages, and trigger cell actions (e.g., reproduce, apoptose, etc.).
Tagged genome modules activate in response to local events (e.g., neighbor actions, incoming messages, etc.), and may be dynamically regulated to support signal-response plasticity.
Instantiating multicells within a unified spatial realm with dense biotic interaction introduces potential for biotic selection feedbacks because multicells constitute each other's environmental context ($f$).
}
