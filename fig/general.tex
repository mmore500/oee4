\caption{%
\textbf{Niche construction drives evolution of complexity across replicates.}
\footnotesize
Across original replicates ($n=40$), fitness advantage of evolved complexity depends on self-acting selection (GEE, interaction coef. 0.1101, $p < 0.001$; panel \ref{fig:general:gee-base}, 95\% CI bands).
This relationship remains consistent after excluding case study strain (GEE, interaction coef. 0.145, $p = 0.003$; Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:general-supp:exclude-co-evo}) or disaggregating per-replicate point-in-time samples within replicates (autoregressive GEE; $p = 0.014$; Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:general-supp:disagg-co-evo}).
Across additional replicates ($n=400$), high-complexity replicates (top 5\% by genotype/phenotype complexity joint exceedance; shaded) disproportionately involve niche construction (Fisher's exact test, 18/2 vs. 216/164 over dashed line; $p = 0.004$; panel \ref{fig:general:pgcomplex-ecoself}).
This relationship remains consistent at 2.5\% and 7.5\% thresholds (9/1 vs. 225/165, $p=0.056$; 28/5 vs. 206/161, $p = 0.001$; Supplementary Figures \labelcref{fig:general-supp:pgcomplex-ecoself-n10,fig:general-supp:pgcomplex-ecoself-n30}) and with no-background fitness as baseline (Supplementary Figures \labelcref{fig:general-supp-nobb:n10,fig:general-supp-nobb:n20,fig:general-supp-nobb:n30}).
High-complexity replicates exhibit diverse morphologies, most differing from cigar morphology of case study (17/20; \url{https://hopth.us/gz}).
