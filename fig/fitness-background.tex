\caption{%
\textbf{Case study strain develops sustained fitness advantage from self-context.}
\footnotesize
Fitness is measured as mean end-state prevalence outcome across ``tournament'' of specimen competition experiments between case study strain and focal strain from other independent evolutionary replicates.
For background type ``none,'' competition is initialized 50/50 between compared strains (Figure \ref{fig:fit:nobbg}).
Otherwise, competition is initialized 25/25 between compared strains, with remaining half initialized as a population sample from either (1) the case study strain's co-evolved background strain (``ecological'') or (2) the case study strain itself (Figure \ref{fig:fit:bbg}).
Fitness measures remain generally similar across background contexts until approximately stint 15, coincident with early observations of morph e (panel \ref{fig:fitness-background:timeseries}, shaded bands as bootstrap 95\% CI).
From this point onwards, self context affords significantly higher tournament fitness than ecological context (Mann-Whitney $U$ test, $p<10^{-25}$).
By contrast, ecological context more closely tracks background-free tournament fitness, though exhibiting strong episodic deviations.
Absent background context, the case study strain's tournament fitness fell below null expectation of 0.5 ($\mu=0.39$; $[0.31,\;0.43]$ bootstrap 95\% CI, $n=40$).
By contrast, in self-context tournament fitness fell above null expectation ($\mu=0.72$; $[0.69,\;0.76]$ bootstrap 95\% CI, $n=40$).
