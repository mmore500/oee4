\caption{%
\textbf{Co-evolutionary adaptation creates genotype complexity.}
\footnotesize
In round-100 isolate of case study background strain, 17 genome sites exhibited adaptive benefit compared to single-site knockouts when tested with case study strain absent.
However, with case study strain present, adaptive benefit was detected at an additional 15 genome sites.
Co-evolutionary adaptation to case study strain thus accounts for 47\% (15/32) of detected genotype complexity in background strain (panel \ref{fig:ecogwas:sites}).
To assess whether co-evolutionary adaptation contributed to genotype complexity more generally, we screened more broadly for adaptive sites specific to co-evolved context --- and tested whether these sites associated with overall co-evolutionary adaptation.
Among second-round replicates ($n=400$), strains with weak adaptation to co-evolved context (fewer than 10\% competition-outcome flips and less than 5\% net change in comparator-based fitness vs. no-background competitions) were selected for knockout analysis ($n=7$).
For comparison, $n=7$ strains with strongest adaptation to co-evolved context were also subjected to knockout analysis.
To ensure sign-change sensitivity, both samples were restricted to strains with moderate absolute fitness (i.e., no-background comparator-based fitness between 0.25 and 0.75).
Scatterplots pool single-site knockout fitness outcomes across sample strains, with dashed region designating adaptation specific to co-evolved background (fit thresh 0.2/0.45; Panel \ref{fig:ecogwas:ythresh045}, left).
Fitness $>0.5$ indicates competition advantage over wildtype.
Strains with strong adaptation to co-evolved context exhibit the greatest additional genetic complexity in that context (Mann-Whitney test, $p=0.016$, Cliff's $\delta=0.76$; Panel \ref{fig:ecogwas:ythresh045}, right).
This relationship remains consistent under more stringent criteria for neutral fitness (fit thresh 0.2/0.5; Mann-Whitney test, $p=0.013$, Cliff's $\delta=0.78$; Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:ecogwas-supp}).
}
