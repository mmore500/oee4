\textbf{Selection of case study strain.}
\footnotesize
Compared to other replicates, case study 16005 sustained extreme outlying complexity (upper panels \labelcref{fig:16005-timelapse:genotype-complexity,fig:16005-timelapse:phenotype-complexity}) and exhibited unique multi-stage patterned growth (lower panels \labelcref{fig:16005-timelapse:update64,fig:16005-timelapse:update256,fig:16005-timelapse:update832,fig:16005-timelapse:update6720,fig:16005-timelapse:update7616}).
Genotype complexity is count of genome sites contributing to fitness; phenotype complexity count of distinct cell input/output operations contributing to fitness --- both determined via knockout trials.
Chronological snapshots of example multicell life history from case study lineage are shown left-to-right (morph ``$j$,'' stint 100; Supplementary Table \ref{tab:morph_descriptions}).
In snapshots, color hue denotes, and black borders divide, multicell groups.
White borders reflect inner structure.
From initial unicell groups (\ref{fig:16005-timelapse:update64}), cells replicate to form horizontal ``cigar-like'' groups (\labelcref{fig:16005-timelapse:update256,fig:16005-timelapse:update832}).
In a delayed second stage of growth, groups expand vertically to produce a ``box-like'' final morphology (\labelcref{fig:16005-timelapse:update6720,fig:16005-timelapse:update7616}).
During this secondary phase of growth, some groups establish cigar-like propagules (\labelcref{fig:16005-timelapse:update7616}).
