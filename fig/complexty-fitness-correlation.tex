\caption{%
\textbf{Weak relationship between genetic complexity and fitness across independent replicates.}
\footnotesize
Across 40 independent evolutionary replicates, no statistical relationship arose between genetic complexity and fitness (Pearson correlation).
Genetic complexity was measured via single-site knockout assays, and fitness was measured as mean end-state prevalence outcome across ``tournament'' of competition trials with focal strains from other independent evolutionary replicates.
Correlation was weakly negative for fitness measured with no context or strains' co-evolved ecological background context, and marginally positive for fitness measured with self context background (right panel, shaded areas as bootstrap 95\% CI).
(See Figure \ref{fig:fitness-background} for detail of background contexts in fitness measurement.)
To assess whether self context or ecological context drove stronger fitness effects, we compared the distribution of tournament fitness performance measured across replicates under these contexts (left panel).
Self context drove larger fitness benefits, with this effect arising more strongly in high-complexity strains ($p=0.03$, Mann-Whitney $U$ test; Cliff's $\delta$ 0.42) versus lower-complexity strains ($p=0.14$, Mann-Whitney $U$ test; Cliff's $\delta$ 0.28).
Results remain similar after disaggregating stint timepoints within independent replicates, with stronger self-context fitness benefit arising only among high-complexity replicates ($p<10^{-5}$, $\delta=0.26$; Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:complexity-fitness-correlation-unagg}).
Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:external-bb-all} overviews tournament fitness measures for each independent replicate.
}
