\caption{%
\textbf{Relationship between genetic complexity and fitness across independent replicates, disaggregated by timepoint.}
\footnotesize
Disaggregated variant of Figure \ref{fig:complexity-fitness-correlation}, which reports from replicate means taken across assayed stints.
Across 40 independent evolutionary replicates, no statistical relationship arose between genetic complexity and fitness (Pearson correlation).
Genetic complexity was measured via single-site knockout assays, and fitness was measured as mean end-state prevalence outcome across ``tournament'' of competition trials with focal strains from other independent evolutionary replicates.
Correlation between genetic complexity and tournament fitness varies substantially across assayed timepoint (right panel, shaded areas as bootstrap 95\% CI).
(See Figure \ref{fig:fitness-background} for detail of background contexts in fitness measurement.)
Self context drove larger fitness benefits than ecological context (left panel), with this effect arising more strongly in high-complexity strains ($p<10^{-5}$, Mann-Whitney $U$ test; Cliff's $\delta$ 0.26) versus lower-complexity strains ($p=0.27$, Mann-Whitney $U$ test; Cliff's $\delta$ 0.07).
Supplementary Figure \ref{fig:external-bb-all} overviews tournament fitness measures for each independent replicate.
}
