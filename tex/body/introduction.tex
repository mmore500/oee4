\section{Introduction}

% TODO sell why this matters
Why should complex multicellular organisms exist, when simpler unicellular organisms grow biomass with substantially greater efficiency \citep{lynch2024bioenergetic}?
One possible explanation points to feedback from organisms' evolved traits in shaping their own environmental context.
Where new traits become advantaged by such niche construction effects, selection can boost complexity \citep{TODO}.
% Indeed, current theories of complex multicellularity draw heavily on niche construction \citep{TODO}.
In the case of complex multicellularity, substantial observational evidence supports the evolutionary significance of niche construction \citep{TODO} --- backed by equally rigorous theoretical developments \citep{TODO}.
To date, however, experimental approaches have yet to directly test how niche construction affects genetic and phenotypic complexity during early multicell evolution \citep{goldsby2012task,goldsby2014evolutionary,YEASTTOODO,VOLVOXTODO}.

Leveraging an artificial life model to study synthetic instances of \textit{de novo} multicell evolution \textit{in silico}, we find direct evidence that spontaneous niche construction effects can stabilize substantially elevated genetic and phenotypic complexity.
Furthermore, we report, with much greater detail than previously possible, how this process unfolds in a concrete instance.
Critical to the work's tractability is our use of digital organisms as a model system: computer programs that replicate, mutate, compete, and thus evolve --- analogous to genomes within biological cells \citep{pennock2007models}.
Although using an artificial substrate, we frame our approach to illuminate principles relevant across any evolving system \citep{zaman2014coevolution,adami2000evolution,lenski2003evolutionary}.
Indeed, beyond evolutionary biology, the topics of niche construction and Evolutionary Transitions in Individuality (ETI) --- such as multicellularity --- have garnered substantial interest among computer scientists and engineers as means to achieve artificial ``open-ended'' creativity analogous to natural evolution \citep{dolson2019constructive,soros2014identifying,huizinga2018emergence,lehman2012beyond,nguyen2015,stanley2019open,packard2019overview,taylor2016open}.

The next section describes our experimental system, detailing how model digital organisms replicate, form multicells, and interact with other multicells.
Following this, we examine complexity, novelty, and adaptation across the genomic and phenotypic history of a case study lineage exhibiting high genetic complexity, high phenotypic complexity, and morphological innovation.
Further examining this high-complexity lineage, competition assays reveal it evolved to become fit when abundant --- in other words, when serving as its own environmental context.
Using further competition assays, we next test for feedback between this selection and this niche construction effect.
Lastly, we use replay experiments along the high-complexity lineage's history to test the causal role of niche construction in maintaining complexity.
