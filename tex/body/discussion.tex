Complexity and novelty are not inevitable outcomes of Darwinian evolution \citep{stanley2017open}.
Instead, how and why some multicellular lineages evolve novel traits and complex functionality merits explanation.
% within some systems, under some conditions
For this purpose, artificial model systems allow arbitrary sets of mechanistic building blocks to be assembled and, in concrete experiments, tested for sufficiency to reproduce outcomes observed in nature.
Here, using digital organisms, we have found that substantial increases in genetic and phenotypic complexity can be stabilized by spontaneous niche construction effects --- under a basic set of mechanistic assumptions (e.g., spatial structure, kin recognition, cell plasticity, etc.), largely shared in common with natural life.
Using a case study lineage exhibiting high sustained complexity, we use replay experiments abating biotic context to directly demonstrate the causal role of niche construction in maintaining evolved complexity --- and trace a detailed play-by-play account of how complexity, novelty, and adaptation arose across historical intermediates.
