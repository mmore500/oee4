\begin{abstract}
Complex multicellularity is foundational to macroscopic life, yet explanations for its emergence remain hotly contested and difficult to test experimentally.
In particular, it is disputed whether expansions in the functional genome content of multicell lineages were driven primarily by positive selection for conferred adaptive benefits or, instead, arose through drift accumulation of arbitrary contingencies --- with some scaffolding later exaptations.
We examined this issue using a model system of digital organisms: computer programs that evolve as they replicate, mutate, and compete.
Across evolutionary trials allowing these digital organisms to form group structures akin to multicellular bodies, high-complexity lineages exhibited no inherent fitness advantage --- consistent with neutralist expectations.
However, closer analysis of a high-complexity lineage producing asymmetrically patterned group morphologies revealed a different evolutionary driver.
This lineage outcompeted other lineages only when present in sufficient density to impose crowding interference on alternate morphologies --- a phenomenon of self-acting niche construction.
Evolutionary replay experiments abating self-context confirmed that the lineage's high complexity was maintained by this niche construction effect.
Testing across replicates for broader association between niche construction and complexity, we found that such self-adapted strains tended to exhibit high genetic and phenotypic complexity --- while, as before, no association arose between complexity and fitness.
These findings illustrate a scenario where functional complexity accumulates in multicell lineages due to environment-scale biotic contingencies imposing adaptive selection on otherwise arbitrary traits.
Such an ecological framing of complex multicellularity notably bridges adaptationist and neutralist hypotheses: when ecological niche construction acts as the ratchet for contingent entrenchment, traits can be stabilized by genuine adaptive benefit, rather than solely by deleterious side effects of loss.
\end{abstract}
